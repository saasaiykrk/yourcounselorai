Adult Psychotherapy Clinical Protocol Matrix

Oct 13, 2026 · @fabeminds

Version 1.1. One treatment format for 73 adult presenting problems in 15 clinical clusters. Every row gives the care level needed, the strength of evidence, a low-intensity first step, the first-line therapy, a measurable success marker and the red flags for escalation. Diagnostic references follow the WHO International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11).

How to use this matrix

Every row sits on top of the same ten steps. Steps 1, 2 and 10 are never skipped.

  1. Consent and confidentiality: obtain informed consent and explain the limits of confidentiality (risk to self or others, child-protection duties under the POCSO Act, court orders). For serious mental illness, inform clients of their rights under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, including advance directives and a nominated representative.
  2. Risk screen at intake and whenever mood drops: C-SSRS (screen version), plus questions about harm to others, abuse or neglect, and substance withdrawal. Any positive screen goes to row A1 first.
  3. Medical rule-out: for mood, anxiety, sleep and cognitive presentations, check (or refer for) thyroid function, anaemia, vitamin B12, other physical illness, and medication or substance effects.
  4. Baseline measures for everyone: the row's screening tool, plus PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C and WHODAS 2.0 (12-item) for functioning.
  5. Cultural formulation: preferred language, the client's own explanation of the problem, family role in decisions, stigma, and religious or spiritual context. Adapt language, examples and family involvement to fit.
  6. Formulation and goals: precipitating, maintaining and protective factors (5P model), shared in plain language, and 2-4 SMART goals tied to the row's success marker.
  7. Match the level and the step: start at the low-intensity first step for mild to moderate presentations with no red flags. Go straight to first-line therapy for moderate to severe presentations or any red flag. Refer on if the row's care level is above your registration and training.
  8. Deliver the first-line therapy with fidelity: use the published manual, with regular supervision. Add adjuncts only for a specific unmet need.
  9. Measurement-based care: re-score the row's tool every session or every 4th session, and WHODAS every 4-6 sessions. If improvement is under 25% by session 6-8, review the formulation, check adherence and alliance, then step up or switch.
  10. Relapse prevention and discharge: a written early-warning signs plan, booster sessions at 1, 3 and 6 months, and a crisis contact (India: Tele-MANAS 14416). For online sessions, confirm the client's location and an emergency contact at the start of every session.

About the tools

Tools marked † are copyrighted and need a paid licence, permission or certified training, especially for commercial use; unmarked tools are free for clinical use as far as known, but confirm licence terms before building them into a product. Most cut-offs were set in Western samples: use validated Hindi, Marathi or other regional-language versions where they exist, and treat cut-offs as guides, not hard thresholds.

Reading the tables: ICD-11 reference points to the WHO category to look up; confirm the code and diagnostic requirements in the official ICD-11 before recording it. Codes beginning with Q are Chapter 24 factors (reasons for contact that are not disorders). Level · Evidence gives the care level and evidence grade. Step 1 is the low-intensity option for mild to moderate cases. First-line therapy includes the usual number of sessions; the real dose follows the measures. Medication is always prescribed and managed by a psychiatrist or physician.
