Therapy Comparison Chart

Eight approaches, compared on the same points, so that any practitioner can see quickly what each one is for, how it works and when to take care. Everything here is drawn from the full YourCounselor modules and simplified for quick reference.

How to use this chart

  1. Start from the formulation and the client’s goals, not from a favourite approach.
  2. Read the row for each approach under At a glance.
  3. Pick two or three approaches and compare them side by side.
  4. Check Choosing a starting point and the “Take care when” row, then your own competence.
  5. Open the full module before applying an approach with a client.

This chart describes general patterns. Practice varies by protocol, setting and client. It is a starting point for clinical reasoning, not a rule for choosing treatment.

Common misunderstanding“It is positive thinking.” The aim is balanced, believable thoughts tested against evidence.“Just keep busy.” BA targets what avoidance does and links action to values.“Flooding until the fear goes.” Exposure is planned and consented; success is new learning.“Just accept it.” Acceptance concerns inner experience, never harm or injustice.“It is a skills group.” Comprehensive DBT is a coordinated treatment with several parts.“A way to get compliance.” MI respects autonomy, including the choice not to change.“It is just listening.” It is a disciplined way of being, checked with the client.“Look on the bright side.” Distress is acknowledged first; solutions come from the client.

What all eight share

The approaches differ in method, but every module asks the practitioner to hold the same professional ground.

Safety first

Risk assessment, safeguarding and emergency pathways come before any technique, in every approach.

Shared understanding

Each approach works from a formulation or working agreement built with the client, not imposed on them.

Consent and choice

Methods are explained, offered with a rationale, and can be declined or paused.

Cultural humility

Ask, do not assume. Family, faith, language and community matter, and structural barriers are not personal failings.

Measure and adjust

Progress is reviewed with the client and validated measures where appropriate; the plan changes when it does not fit.

Competence and supervision

Work within training, use supervision, and refer when a presentation needs specialist care.

The client’s own words

Every module asks the practitioner to use the client’s language for distress, goals and change.

Combining approaches

  • The relationship qualities described in Person-Centred Therapy, empathy, genuineness and acceptance, support every other approach.
  • Motivational Interviewing is often used to build engagement before or alongside CBT, BA, exposure or DBT.
  • Behavioural activation and exposure are core methods inside CBT, as well as approaches in their own right.
  • CBT and ACT both use behaviour change; the main difference is that CBT tests the content of thoughts, while ACT changes the person’s relationship with them.
  • SFBT questions such as best hopes and scaling can frame goals in any approach.
  • When combining, name the model, keep one clear formulation, check competence, and avoid a mix that confuses the client.
