### 5. Best-Suited Therapy Recommendation

**Primary approach: CBT with ERP, delivered with active parent involvement. HRT is added for hair picking, and graded exposure for darkness.** Introduce one new target at a time — starting everything at once overwhelms a 9-year-old and his family.

**Order of priority:** (1) engagement, assessment and psychoeducation → (2) ERP on the easiest rituals + parents start reducing accommodation → (3) HRT for hair picking → (4) darkness ladder → (5) relapse prevention. The screen plan runs alongside from Week 3, reduced gradually.

#### Step 1 — Externalise the OCD (Sessions 1–2)

- Help the child give the OCD a name (e.g., "Bossy Brain"). This separates him from the problem: *"It's not you, it's Bossy Brain making you do it."*
- Teach the **fear thermometer** (0 = calm, 10 = most scared or uncomfortable). He will rate every exposure with it.

#### Step 2 — Build the exposure ladder together (Session 2–3)

List each ritual and avoidance, and have the child rate how hard it would be to resist. Start exposures at about 3–4, never at the top.

| Step | Exposure task | Expected rating (0–10) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Watch one reel to the end without rewinding | 3 |
| 2 | Parent says a word once only, and does not repeat it | 4 |
| 3 | Swallow saliva once, then wait 1 minute before spitting | 4 |
| 4 | Touch a black object and keep holding it for 2 minutes | 5 |
| 5 | Watch 5 reels in a row without rewinding any | 6 |
| 6 | Step on an "unlucky" floor spot and stay there | 7 |
| 7 | Swallow saliva normally for 30 minutes with no spitting | 8 |
| 8 | Wear or carry something black all day at school | 9 |

#### Step 3 — Run each exposure (the ERP routine)

1. **Predict:** ask *"What do you think will happen if you don't do the ritual?"* and *"How sure are you, out of 10?"*
2. **Do it:** the child does the task and does **not** do the ritual. The therapist coaches calmly without reassuring ("You're doing it — notice the feeling").
3. **Stay:** remain in the situation until the prediction has been tested — typically 10–30 minutes — even if the rating stays high.
4. **Review:** *"What actually happened? What did you learn?"* Write it on the exposure record.
5. **Practise:** the same task becomes daily homework until it rates 2 or below, then move up a step.

**Why this matters:** the goal is not to make anxiety disappear in session. It is for the child to learn that the feared outcome doesn't happen and that he can handle the discomfort. Avoid reassurance like "Black doesn't mean bad luck" — in OCD, reassurance quickly becomes another ritual.

#### Step 4 — Habit Reversal Training for hair picking (from Session 5)

1. **Awareness training:** the child describes the movement, and learns his warning signs (e.g., hand moving to head, feeling bored during screen time).
2. **Competing response:** at the urge, he makes fists or squeezes a stress ball for 1 minute or until the urge passes. It must be something that can't be done at the same time as picking.
3. **Social support:** parents notice and praise use of the competing response, and give a gentle agreed prompt ("hands busy") without criticism.
4. **Stimulus control:** keep hands occupied in high-risk moments (fidget item during screen time and homework).

#### Step 5 — Graded exposure for darkness (from Session 7)

Build a separate ladder: bathroom with light on and door half closed → dim light → walk to bathroom alone with a torch → alone without torch → sleep with nightlight only → parent leaves the room before he falls asleep. A nightlight is acceptable for the final goal; complete darkness is not required.

#### Step 6 — Screen-use plan (from Week 3)

- Turn off autoplay and remove the reels/shorts feed where possible; this also removes the main trigger for rewinding.
- Reduce daily use by about 15 minutes per week toward the family's agreed target, rather than a sudden ban (sudden bans often cause outbursts and secret use).
- Screens only after homework and outdoor play; no screens in the bedroom or in the hour before bed.
- Roblox time is earned through a reward chart, not used as a calming tool during anxiety.

### 6. Therapist's Role & Actions

The therapist acts as a **coach**: warm, playful with the child, firm about not doing rituals, and the person who teaches parents to do the same at home.

#### Key actions

- Build rapport first. Use drawings, games, and the "Bossy Brain" language so therapy feels like a team effort.
- Explain OCD to parents simply: *"Rituals bring short-term relief, which teaches the brain the ritual was needed. Each time a ritual is skipped, the brain learns it wasn't."*
- Model exposures in session before asking parents to run them at home.
- Review homework at the start of every session. Praise effort, not just success.
- Measure at Weeks 1, 6, and 12, and change the plan if progress stalls.
- Coordinate with the school counsellor (with parental consent) if rituals or avoidance happen at school.
- Seek supervision if you are new to ERP. Bring your exposure ladder and Week 6 scores to supervision.

#### Common mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it backfires | Do this instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reassuring ("Nothing bad will happen") | Becomes a new ritual; the child keeps needing it | "Let's find out what happens." |
| Starting exposures too high on the ladder | Child refuses or drops out | Start at a rating of 3–4 |
| Ending an exposure early because the child is upset | Teaches that escape is needed | Stay calm, stay in the task, praise courage |
| Replacement behaviours for compulsions | Creates a new ritual | Use ERP for compulsions; HRT only for habits |
| Parents stopping all accommodation in one day | Meltdowns and conflict | Reduce one accommodation at a time, with a plan |

### 7. Client's Actions and Lifestyle Adjustments

Tasks are written for a 9-year-old. Keep them short, visible (on a chart), and rewarded.

- **Daily "brave practice":** 10–15 minutes of the current exposure step, recorded with the fear thermometer.
- **Boss back Bossy Brain:** when an urge comes, say *"Nice try, Bossy Brain, I'm not doing it"* and wait.
- **Hands busy:** use the agreed competing response when the urge to pick hair comes.
- **Sleep routine:** same bedtime, no screens for an hour before bed, nightlight allowed.
- **Move every day:** at least an hour of active outdoor play, which also replaces screen time.
- **Regular meals:** if the saliva ritual affects eating, tell the therapist — this needs a paediatric check.

### 8. Guardian/Parent Guidance

Parents are the most important part of this plan. The child spends one hour a week with the therapist and the rest of the week with them.

#### The core idea: support, not accommodation

Accommodation means doing things that keep the OCD going (repeating words, accompanying to the bathroom, avoiding black items for him). Support means showing you understand his fear **and** believe he can cope.

**Supportive statement to use:** *"I know this feels really hard. I'm not going to repeat it, because I know you can handle this feeling. I'm right here."*

#### Step-by-step accommodation reduction

1. **List** every accommodation (use the FAS-PR answers as a starting list).
2. **Pick one** that is easiest to change, e.g., repeating a word more than once.
3. **Tell the child in advance,** calmly: *"From tomorrow, I'll say things just once. I know you can manage."*
4. **Follow through every time.** Both parents and the sibling must respond the same way.
5. **Once it's stable (about 1–2 weeks), choose the next one.**

#### What to do and not do

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Use the supportive statement, then stay calm | Argue, explain at length, or reassure repeatedly |
| Praise brave attempts specifically ("You watched it once — that was brave") | Punish or shame rituals or hair picking |
| Reward progress with stickers that add up to privileges | Use screen time to calm him down when anxious |
| Keep a daily log for the therapist | Change the plan without telling the therapist |
| Look after yourselves; this is tiring | Blame each other when there's a setback |

**If he gets very upset:** stay nearby and calm, don't give in to the ritual, and wait. Distress usually peaks and settles. If he ever talks about hurting himself or others, or the distress is unmanageable, contact the therapist the same day, or emergency services if anyone is in danger.

### 9. Session-Wise Treatment Plan (12 Weeks)

Weekly sessions of about 60 minutes: roughly 40 minutes with the child (parents join for the last part) and 15–20 minutes with parents. Move to the next step only when the current one is manageable; it is normal for some steps to take two weeks.

#### Phase 1 — Assess and engage (Weeks 1–2)

**Week 1: Assessment and rapport**

- *Session:* confirm risk screening; full history with parents (onset, family history, school); CY-BOCS, SCAS, FAS-PR; rapport games with the child.
- *Homework:* parents start a daily ritual log and screen-time log.
- *Check:* was onset sudden? If yes, paediatric referral before continuing.

**Week 2: Psychoeducation and externalising**

- *Session:* explain OCD to child and parents; name "Bossy Brain"; teach the fear thermometer; SCQ to parents; decide on OT referral.
- *Homework:* child notices and rates "Bossy Brain moments" on a simple chart.
- *Parent task:* list all accommodations; do not change anything yet.

#### Phase 2 — Start ERP and parent change (Weeks 3–6)

**Week 3: Build the ladder and first exposure**

- *Session:* build the exposure ladder; first in-session exposure (watch one reel without rewinding); start the screen plan (autoplay off).
- *Homework:* daily practice of step 1.
- *Parent task:* reduce accommodation #1 (say things once only).

**Week 4: Climb the ladder**

- *Session:* review records; exposures for steps 2–3 (repeating words, saliva delay).
- *Homework:* daily practice of current step; reward chart running.
- *Parent task:* consistent response from both parents and sibling; use the supportive statement.

**Week 5: Add HRT for hair picking**

- *Session:* awareness training and competing response; continue ERP at step 3–4 (black objects).
- *Homework:* ERP practice + "hands busy" practice; tally picking episodes daily.
- *Parent task:* praise the competing response; reduce accommodation #2.

**Week 6: Mid-point review**

- *Session:* repeat CY-BOCS and FAS-PR; compare with Week 1; continue ERP.
- *Decision:* if CY-BOCS has dropped, continue. If no change, check homework completion and parental accommodation first, then discuss with a supervisor.
- *Parent task:* review progress with the therapist and agree the next two accommodations to reduce.

#### Phase 3 — Harder steps and darkness fear (Weeks 7–10)

**Week 7: Darkness ladder**

- *Session:* build the darkness ladder; first step in session (dim room with therapist outside the door); ERP continues on steps 5–6.
- *Homework:* one darkness step nightly + ERP practice.
- *Parent task:* stop accompanying to the bathroom, starting with daytime trips.

**Week 8: Unlucky spots and black-omen fears**

- *Session:* exposure to "unlucky" floor spots and black items; practise not seeking reassurance.
- *Homework:* daily black-object exposure at home.
- *Parent task:* respond to reassurance requests with the supportive statement only.

**Week 9: Top of the ladder**

- *Session:* hardest saliva step (30 minutes no spitting); combine exposures in real-life settings (school bag, outing).
- *Homework:* "exposure in daily life" — child chooses one brave task per day.
- *Parent task:* reduce remaining accommodations.

**Week 10: Independence**

- *Session:* child plans his own exposures (therapist steps back); review screen plan.
- *Homework:* child-designed exposures; sleep with nightlight only.

#### Phase 4 — Relapse prevention (Weeks 11–12)

**Week 11: Relapse prevention plan**

- *Session:* make a "Bossy Brain battle plan": early warning signs, what to do, who to tell. Explain that small setbacks are normal ("lapse, not relapse").
- *Parent task:* write what they'll do if old rituals return.

**Week 12: Final review**

- *Session:* repeat CY-BOCS, SCAS, FAS-PR; celebrate progress (certificate); agree follow-up.
- *Decision:* if response is good, move to booster sessions at 2, 4, and 8 weeks. If limited, extend ERP and recommend child psychiatry consultation.
