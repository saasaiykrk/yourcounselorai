## PART 24 — COUPLES THERAPY FRAMEWORK & ASSESSMENT (CPL)

### CPL-00 Core model
Five ideas from the couple-therapy research literature shape every module:
1. **Balance of positives and negatives:** distressed couples exchange more negative and fewer positive behaviours than satisfied couples (e.g., Jacobson & Margolin; Gottman). So therapy both *builds positives* (care, fun, appreciation) and *reduces negatives* (criticism, escalation).
2. **Change your own part (self-regulation; Halford):** each partner decides what *they* will do differently, plans it, carries it out, checks how it went and adjusts. This replaces efforts to change the other person.
3. **Judge by outcome (after McCullough's CBASP):** a thought or action is evaluated by whether it moved the person towards the outcome they wanted, rather than labelled "irrational".
4. **Acceptance alongside change (IBCT; Christensen & Jacobson):** some differences between partners are lasting, so change work is combined with building acceptance.
5. **Partner-involved treatment of individual problems:** relationship difficulties often sit alongside depression, anxiety, alcohol use or eating problems. Use couple-based versions of evidence-based individual treatments.

### CPL-01 Evidence grades
| Approach | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Behavioural / cognitive-behavioural couple therapy (BCT / CBCT) | **A** |
| Integrative behavioural couple therapy (IBCT) | **A** |
| Emotionally focused couple therapy (EFT; Core 3 PDX-07) | **A** |
| **Behavioural couples therapy for alcohol and drug use** (O'Farrell & Fals-Stewart) | **A** — better abstinence and relationship outcomes, and less partner violence, than individual treatment alone |
| Couple therapy for depression (CBCT; partner-assisted) | **B–A**; strongest when relationship distress and depression occur together |
| Partner-assisted CBT / exposure for anxiety and OCD | **B** |
| Family-based treatment for eating disorders | **A** in adolescents; **C** for adult couples |
| Integrative treatment after an affair (Baucom, Gordon & Snyder) | **B** |
| Conjoint treatment for **situational** couple violence (low severity, no fear) | **B** |
| Conjoint treatment for **coercive controlling violence** | ⚠ **Contraindicated.** Work with each person separately |
| Parenting programmes (e.g., Triple P, Parent Management Training) | **A** |
| Sensate focus and sex therapy | **B** |

### CPL-02 Assessment protocol
**Sequence:**
1. Joint session
2. **One individual session with each partner — mandatory.** Violence, affairs and fear often emerge only when the other partner is absent.
3. Joint feedback session with a shared formulation

**Areas to cover:**
- How the relationship began and developed; satisfaction then and now
- Commitment, and any steps already taken towards separating
- Strengths and good times
- Main areas of conflict and how the couple communicates
- Each partner's mental health
- Alcohol and drug use
- **Violence and abuse** — physical, emotional and sexual; ask about fear and safety *privately*
- Affairs, or suspicion of one
- Children and parenting
- Money
- Extended family and in-laws
- Culture and religion
- Sexual relationship

**Measures:**

| Construct | Tool | Notes / licence |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship satisfaction | **Couples Satisfaction Index (CSI-32 / 16 / 4; Funk & Rogge, 2007)** | Free for use; sensitive to change — suits in-app tracking |
| Satisfaction (alternatives) | Revised Dyadic Adjustment Scale (RDAS-14); Kansas Marital Satisfaction Scale (3 items) | Check licence terms |
| Broad relationship distress | Marital Satisfaction Inventory–Revised (MSI-R) | PUB |
| Steps towards separation | Marital Status Inventory (Weiss & Cerreto) | Research use |
| Changes each partner wants | Areas of Change Questionnaire | Research use |
| Partner violence | **Revised Conflict Tactics Scales (CTS2; Straus et al.)**, completed **separately** by each partner | PUB. ⚠ Never raise one partner's answers in front of the other without a safety plan first |
| Alcohol | **AUDIT** (WHO; 10 items; ≥ 8 suggests hazardous use) | Free (WHO) |
| Depression / anxiety | PHQ-9, GAD-7, DASS-21 (Core 1) | Free |
| Sexual function | FSFI, IIEF | Research use — check terms |

**Formulation output:**
- Strengths
- Main themes
- The couple's recurring cycle (CPL-07)
- Each partner's individual contributions
- Safety status
- Recommended format (joint, individual or combined)

### CPL-03 Starting agreements and ground rules
- **What kind of therapy** (the couple chooses explicitly):
  - (a) **Relationship-improvement therapy**
  - (b) **Decision therapy** — for couples unsure whether to stay together
  - (c) **Separation therapy** — to part as constructively as possible
- **No-secrets policy:** the therapist will not keep secrets that undermine the joint work (e.g., an ongoing affair). Explain this at intake, along with how information from individual sessions will be handled.
- **Safety agreements:**
  - No threats of self-harm or of harming anyone else.
  - While therapy continues, no threats to leave or to be sexually involved with someone else.
  - No physical aggression under any circumstances.
  - Either partner can pause a discussion at home that is heating up (CPL-07).
  - What is said in session is not used as ammunition at home.
  - If a session turns destructive, both follow the therapist's lead.
- **Flexible format:** explain *at the outset* that individual sessions may be used whenever anger or safety requires it, so a switch later doesn't feel like a punishment.
- **No alcohol or drugs** before or during sessions.
- **Screen each partner individually** for risk of suicide or of harming others.

### CPL-04 My-Part Plan (core homework in most modules)
Based on Halford's self-regulation approach; the worksheet design is our own.
1. **My behaviour:** one specific thing *I* do that I want to change.
2. **Costs and benefits:** what it gets me now, and what it costs me and us.
3. **New behaviour:** exactly what I will do instead.
4. **Plan:** when, where and how I'll do it; what might get in the way.
5. **Review:** rate how well I followed the plan (0–10) and adjust it.

**Rule:** each partner plans only for their **own** behaviour — never for managing the other's.

### CPL-05 Outcome-Check worksheet (adapted from McCullough's situational analysis, CBASP)
**Part 1 — describe it:**
1. **What happened:** one specific episode with a clear start and finish, told as neutrally as a camera would record it.
2. **What I told myself:** 2–4 thoughts or interpretations.
3. **What I did:** words, tone and body language.
4. **What actually resulted:** described in observable terms.
5. **What I wanted to happen:** realistic, *achievable* and *within my control*.
6. **Did I get it?** Yes / No.

**Part 2 — repair it:**
- Check each thought with three questions:
  - **Is it about this situation** (not a sweeping generalisation)?
  - **Does the evidence support it?**
  - **Does it help me get what I wanted?**
  Rewrite any thought that fails.
- Rewrite what I did.
- If what I wanted was unrealistic or not in my control, rewrite that too.
- **Take-away:** one lesson to apply in similar situations.

Well suited to a guided in-app form. It complements the Core 2 STR record but is anchored to outcomes, which makes it particularly useful for couple conflict, work–home strain, retirement, religious differences and parenting.

### CPL-06 Communication training
**Two guiding principles — Clarity and Care:**
- **Clarity:** be specific; share both thoughts and feelings; pay attention; ask questions to check understanding; summarise what you heard, including feelings.
- **Care:** mention positives; soften how negatives are said; show you are listening even when you disagree; hold back judgement.

**Practice:** begin with an easy topic; afterwards each partner rates themselves (0–10) on clarity and on care.

**Core skills:**

| Skill | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Specific positive requests** | Ask for something observable and for *more* of a positive behaviour rather than less of a negative one ("Could you message me if you'll be home after 9?" rather than "Stop being so thoughtless") |
| **Speaker–listener turns** (after Markman & Stanley's PREP) | The speaker says a short "I" statement; the listener sums it up until the speaker agrees they've got it; then they swap. No counter-argument until the speaker feels understood |
| **Support or solve?** | Agree at the start whether this talk is for letting off steam (listen and support) or for fixing a problem |
| **Connection time** | 10–15 minutes, several times a week, for personal sharing — not logistics |
| **Saying yes and no to requests** | Practise agreeing to, and respectfully declining, requests for shared activities |
| **Raising a problem gently** | Check timing ("Is now OK to talk about the weekend?"); choose the place; one issue at a time; start softly (Gottman's "softened start-up") |
| **Problem-solving** | Define one problem precisely → list ideas without criticising them → weigh pros and cons → choose → agree who does what and when → review |

### CPL-07 Calming conflict
- **The shared trap:** each partner's way of coping sets off the other (e.g., one withdraws to calm down; the other hears "you don't care" and pursues harder). Map the cycle together and rename it as a trap they are both caught in.
- **Big-picture vs specific:** small disagreements get treated as proof of big themes ("you don't love me"). Bring the talk back to the specific issue.
- **Structured time-out: Pause → Settle → Return**
  1. Agree a time-out signal in advance.
  2. Pause: take a break for an agreed period (e.g., 20–60 minutes).
  3. Settle: calm the body (slow breathing, a walk); no alcohol; no driving while very angry.
  4. Reflect: use calming self-talk or the Outcome-Check worksheet.
  5. **Return:** come back at the agreed time and continue or re-schedule.
  The return step stops time-out being used as punishment or abandonment.
- **Early warning signs:** each partner lists their first signs of escalation and agreed responses (lower voice, sit down, take a break, reflect back).
- **Flooding (Gottman):** once heart rate and arousal are very high, productive talk is unlikely — a sign to pause.

### CPL-08 Anger module for couples
- **Anger shows up in three ways:** body sensations and feelings, thoughts, and actions.
- **Anger thermometer 0–100 with zones** (for the in-app tracker):
  - 0–30 **Green** — calm to mildly annoyed
  - 31–50 **Yellow** — noticeably angry; use calming skills
  - **51–70 Amber — take a time-out now**
  - **71–100 Red** — high risk of aggression; leave the situation safely
- **What angry behaviour is often trying to achieve:**
  1. Getting a need met (attention, one's way) — or expressing frustration at not getting it
  2. Protecting independence (reacting to feeling controlled)
  3. Protecting oneself (reacting to hurt, threat or feeling vulnerable)
  4. Releasing built-up stress
  Log each episode by function, then find non-aggressive ways to meet the same need.
- **Hard and soft emotions** (after IBCT):
  - Protective emotions such as anger or contempt usually sit on top of *softer* ones — hurt, fear, sadness, disappointment.
  - Coach each partner to name the soft feeling and the need ("I felt hurt and left out when…, and I'd like…") instead of attacking.
  - The listener reflects back the vulnerability.
- **Assertive, passive and aggressive:** learn to tell them apart, then rehearse assertive expression; the receiving partner reflects back reasonable anger.
- **Respectful-anger agreement:** each partner has the right to express anger and the responsibility to consider the other's feelings while doing so.

### CPL-09 Acceptance techniques (IBCT; Christensen & Jacobson)
| Technique | What it involves |
|---|---|
| **Empathic joining** | Re-describe the problem in terms of each partner's softer feelings and understandable reasons |
| **Unified detachment** | Treat the recurring pattern as an "it" outside both of them (e.g., "the chase-and-hide routine") and study it together, without blame |
| **Building tolerance** | (a) Find the *positive side* of a difficult trait (e.g., his insistence on planning = wanting the family to be secure); (b) **deliberately act out the problem pattern in session** while calm; (c) **fake the pattern at home** when calm and talk about the feelings it brings up; (d) prepare for the pattern to return and plan self-care |
| **Self-care** | Meet some needs outside the relationship (friends, interests) so the relationship doesn't carry everything |
| **Accepting deeper differences** | Keep track of which ways of adapting to a core difference work and which don't |

### CPL-10 Building positives
- **Daily Positives Log:** each day, each partner notes at least one thing the partner did that they appreciated and at least one thing they did to please the partner; bring the log to sessions (after Jacobson & Margolin's behaviour-exchange methods).
- **Caring actions** (after Stuart's "caring days"): each partner lists small, specific things that please them (a cup of tea brought unasked, a message during the day, taking over a chore). The other does several each week, without keeping score.
- **Shared enjoyable activities:**
  - Brainstorm three lists: things to do *alone*, *together* and *with others*.
  - Schedule something every week.
  - Take turns choosing.
  - Now and then, plan a special day around one partner's preferences.
- **Appreciation:** say thank you and give genuine compliments daily.
- **Principle: feelings often follow actions.** In distressed relationships, warm feelings usually return *after* positive behaviour increases, not before. Set realistic expectations for when affection will come back.

### CPL-11 Working with the sexual relationship (common core)
- **Assess:** sexual history; what has been good; how expectations have changed; medical conditions and medications (refer to a doctor); body image; trauma; religious and family messages about sex; attraction to someone else; questions of sexual orientation (explored without judgement); self-esteem.
- **Ground rule:** sex is always voluntary — no pressure, threats or criticism of the partner's sexuality.
- **Psycho-education:** desire and frequency vary normally between people and across life; sexual functioning is easily disrupted by conflict or pressure; a better relationship usually comes before better sex.
- **Sensate focus (Masters & Johnson), step by step:**
  - Step 1: partners take turns giving and receiving non-sexual touch, *avoiding breasts and genitals*, with an agreed ban on intercourse.
  - Step 2: include breast and genital touch.
  - Step 3: move gradually towards intercourse.
  - Review each step in session and adjust based on both partners' feedback.
- **Other methods:** encourage sexual thoughts and fantasy between encounters; educational reading; for vaginismus or pain with penetration, gradual self-controlled use of dilators after gynaecological assessment; pause any activity that triggers trauma memories until they have been worked through.
- Refer to a qualified sex therapist, urologist or gynaecologist as needed.

### CPL-12 Other formats
- **Only one partner attends (CP-ONE):**
  - Ask permission to invite the other partner.
  - Screen for violence and affairs.
  - Focus on what the attending partner can change (My-Part Plans, Outcome-Check worksheets, acceptance, enjoyable activities with and without the partner, meeting needs outside the relationship).
  - Weigh the pros and cons of staying.
- **Individual sessions during couple therapy:** for high anger or volatility, violence screening, and writing letters after an affair.
- **Children in sessions:** only to tell them about a separation or support them through it, or to hear their needs; never ask them to take sides.

## PART 25 — SAFETY: PARTNER VIOLENCE AND COERCION (CPL-20s)

### CPL-20 Types of partner violence (this decides the format)
Based on the typology developed by Michael P. Johnson and by Kelly & Johnson.

| Type | Features | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Coercive controlling violence ("intimate terrorism")** | A one-sided pattern of control — physical, emotional or sexual; isolating the partner; monitoring; restricting money; stalking; **the victim is afraid** | ⚠ **No joint therapy.** Separate help for each: a perpetrator programme for the person using violence; advocacy and safety planning for the victim. Refer to police and legal services |
| **Situational couple violence** | Grows out of escalating arguments; both may use force; lower severity; **no fear and no pattern of control** | Joint therapy only *if* screening confirms no fear, no injury, no coercion, both partners want it, and a safety plan is in place |
| **Emotional / psychological abuse** | Insults, name-calling, humiliation, threats (to leave, to harm, to be unfaithful), isolation, restricting movement, demanding accounts of time, days of silent treatment, false accusations | Treat as abuse and apply the safety steps |
| **Sexual coercion** | Pressure, threats or force for sex; demeaning sexual criticism; threats to withhold sex or to have sex with others | Treat as abuse — usually occurs with emotional and physical abuse |

### CPL-21 Partner-violence protocol
1. **Screen each partner privately** (CTS2 plus interview): what led up to incidents, impact, **purpose**, **fear**, injuries, weapons, strangulation, threats to kill, stalking, pregnancy, children exposed, substance use, and whether it is safe to discuss this jointly.
2. **Decide on risk level:** if violence is uncontrolled, or either partner fears the other → **individual work only** until safe.
3. **Safety plan for the person at risk:** warning signs, escape route, emergency bag, safe place, emergency contacts, and how to keep children safe.
4. **Non-violence agreement:** no abuse or intimidation *regardless of any claimed provocation*.
5. **Substance-use assessment** for both partners.
6. **Psycho-education:**
   - Clear, shared definitions of physical, emotional and sexual abuse.
   - The effects of abuse on the victim, the person using abuse, and **children**.
   - Power and control; gender norms.
7. **Work with the person using violence:**
   - Full responsibility, no blaming ("She made me do it" is not accepted).
   - Identify the feelings underneath controlling behaviour.
   - Identify the beliefs that justify violence.
   - List the costs of controlling behaviour.
   - Write a plan of alternatives (time-out, calling a friend, walking, writing, slow breathing).
   - Use the Outcome-Check worksheet for escalating situations.
8. **Joint skills work (situational couple violence only):** anger thermometer, time-out, communication and problem-solving; rehearse alternatives to the patterns that fuel aggression.
9. **Forgiveness** is a *process*, not a moment. Never press the victim to forgive.
10. **Consider alternatives to the relationship;** refer for respite, shelter or legal help.

**Indian law and help (verify before release):**
- Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 — protection orders, residence rights, Protection Officers.
- Cruelty by a husband or his relatives — Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (formerly IPC s. 498A).
- Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.
- Helplines: **181** (Women Helpline), **112** (emergency), National Commission for Women helpline, One Stop Centres (Sakhi).
- **Clinically, any sex without consent is abuse, whatever its legal status.**

## PART 26 — PROBLEM MODULES (CP)
**Card layout:** **Presentation** → **Goals** → **Objectives** (measurable templates written to the Core 3 TPE-02 standard; adjust numbers per couple) → **Methods** → **⚠ Notes**. "Each" means each partner.

### Individual difficulties within the couple

**CP-ALC Alcohol or drug use (behavioural couples therapy; O'Farrell & Fals-Stewart)**
- **Presentation:** heavy use by one or both partners; arguments about drinking; repeated promises to stop that aren't kept; aggression when intoxicated; a worn-down relationship; the other partner covering up, making excuses or trying to keep the peace.
- **Goals:** stop or greatly reduce use; support recovery together; communicate better and enjoy time together; the other partner stops enabling and speaks up assertively; no violence.
- **Objectives:**
  - Both partners complete the AUDIT (or a drug-use screen) by session 2.
  - Sign a non-violence agreement and a safety plan by session 2.
  - Attend every session alcohol- and drug-free.
  - The person using lists at least 3 things alcohol/drugs are used for (e.g., relaxing, confidence, escape) and a non-substance alternative for each, by week 4.
  - Hold a brief daily **recovery check-in** at an agreed time for 12 weeks: the person using states their intention not to use that day; the partner thanks them and does not raise past use. Log completion ≥ 6 days a week.
  - Where prescribed, take recovery medication (e.g., disulfiram, naltrexone) under medical supervision, with the partner observing if agreed.
  - Attend a self-help or recovery group at least weekly for 3 months.
  - Complete at least 2 shared substance-free activities per week for 8 weeks.
  - The partner identifies 3 enabling behaviours by week 4 and stops at least 2 of them by week 10.
  - Draw up a written household budget together by week 8.
  - Each lists 3 substance-free social contacts or settings by week 6.
  - If use cannot be controlled, move to a higher level of care within 1 week of agreed warning signs.
- **Methods:** BCT, relapse prevention, communication and problem-solving training (CPL-06), positives (CPL-10), making amends to the family.
- **⚠:** risk of partner violence; alcohol withdrawal may need medical management; screen the other partner too.

**CP-ANX Anxiety (partner-assisted)**
- **Presentation:** anxiety limits one partner's roles; arguments about the anxiety or the changes the couple makes around it; shrinking social life; long, repeated conversations about worries.
- **Goals:** learn CBT coping skills; support each other in helpful ways; practise exposure together; think more realistically; share stress-reducing activities; keep worry talk contained.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each writes how the anxiety affects them and the relationship, by week 2.
  - Together list at least 5 ways the partner's "help" (reassurance, avoiding things) may keep the anxiety going, by week 4.
  - The partner acts as a **coping coach** in at least 1 exposure practice per week for 8 weeks (Core 1 INT-03; Core 2 CSK-35).
  - Agree a discreet 0–10 distress code for use in public by week 3.
  - Practise slow breathing daily for 10 minutes, 5 days a week, for 6 weeks.
  - Complete 1 exposure step per week without safety behaviours, for 10 weeks.
  - Limit worry discussions to 1 scheduled "worry time" of no more than 20 minutes per day, for 6 weeks.
  - Plan at least 1 shared relaxing activity per week.
  - Spend 5 minutes per week talking positively about plans for the future.

**CP-DEP-R Depression connected with relationship problems** (after Beach's marital discord model)
- **Presentation:** one partner is depressed and believes relationship problems are a cause; there may have been a damaging event (e.g., abuse, betrayal).
- **Goals:** improve relationship satisfaction; lift mood and energy; restore self-esteem; prevent relapse.
- **Objectives:**
  - Both complete the CSI and the depressed partner the PHQ-9 at baseline and every 4 weeks.
  - Together draw a timeline showing which came first, depression or relationship problems, by week 3.
  - Complete a written suicide-prevention safety plan by session 2 (Core 1 ASM-06).
  - Reduce critical or hostile comments to each other by half over 6 weeks (each keeps a tally).
  - The depressed partner shares low feelings in an agreed, time-limited way (e.g., 10 minutes, 3 times a week) for 6 weeks.
  - Where relevant, the other partner acknowledges and apologises for a specific past failure of support, by week 8.
  - Schedule at least 3 positive shared activities per week (behavioural activation) for 8 weeks.
  - Complete a medication review referral within 4 weeks if indicated.
- **Evidence:** CBCT is effective where depression and relationship distress coexist.

**CP-DEP-I Depression not caused by the relationship (partner-assisted)**
- **Goals:** reduce depression; work through any grief; prevent relapse; make good use of the partner's support.
- **Objectives:**
  - Complete the PHQ-9 every 2 weeks, with results discussed together.
  - Complete a safety plan by session 2.
  - The non-depressed partner describes, in their own words, why depression is not a choice or a lack of effort, by week 4.
  - Agree what will be shared with family and friends, by week 4.
  - Daily social contact and 20 minutes of exercise on at least 5 days a week for 6 weeks.
  - The partner makes 1 brief supportive contact during the working day, 3 times a week.
  - Take medication as prescribed and report side-effects at each session.
- **⚠:** check whether the partner is supportive or critical — a critical partner can undermine treatment, so adjust how much they take part.

**CP-ED Eating difficulties within the couple**
- **Presentation:** bingeing and purging or restriction; distorted body image; physical risk; avoidance of affection and sex; arguments about food; low mood in either partner.
- **Goals:** shared understanding; normal eating and a healthy weight; less conflict over food; a supportive partner; better communication, affection and sexual relationship.
- **Objectives:**
  - Complete a medical assessment (including blood tests such as electrolytes) within 2 weeks.
  - Referral to a specialist eating-disorder service within 4 weeks.
  - The partner stops commenting on or monitoring food and weight entirely, from week 2.
  - The partner gives at least 1 compliment per day about qualities unrelated to appearance, for 6 weeks.
  - The partner shares feelings of frustration or helplessness in session in a non-blaming way.
  - The client describes the kind of support they find helpful, by week 4.
  - Explore perfectionism in the relationship and in sex in at least 2 sessions.
- **⚠:** medical risk and weight determine the level of care; specialist team leads treatment.

**CP-DEPEND Over-dependence in the relationship**
- **Presentation:** self-worth hinges on the partner's words; fear of being left; difficulty deciding anything alone; repeated reassurance-seeking and contact (e.g., many calls at work); jealousy of the partner's colleagues; a history of going quickly from one relationship to the next.
- **Goals:** build self-confidence; cope with the partner's normal absences; make everyday decisions independently; widen sources of self-worth.
- **Objectives:**
  - Log reassurance-seeking for 1 week, then reduce it by half over 6 weeks.
  - The other partner offers unprompted appreciation daily for 6 weeks (reducing the need to ask).
  - Make 1 everyday decision per day without consulting the partner, for 4 weeks.
  - List 5 people or activities outside the relationship that support wellbeing, and contact/do 2 each week.
  - Respect agreed private areas of the partner's life (e.g., no checking messages) for 8 weeks.
  - Practise 1 assertive statement per week for 6 weeks.
  - If the pattern appears across many relationships, consider Core 3 PD-DEP.

### Relationship-process problems

**CP-COM Communication problems**
- **Presentation:** frequent upsetting arguments; problems left unresolved; misunderstandings; small issues turned into big ones; positives rarely acknowledged.
- **Objectives:**
  - Complete 1 speaker–listener practice on a low-conflict topic per week for 4 weeks, then on a medium topic for 4 weeks.
  - Each rates their own clarity and care (0–10) after each practice.
  - Use the Pause → Settle → Return time-out on every occasion an argument reaches the amber zone, for 8 weeks.
  - Complete the Daily Positives Log on at least 5 days a week.
- **Methods:** CPL-06, CPL-07 and CPL-10 in full.

**CP-BLM Blame**
- **Presentation:** one partner holds the other responsible for everything and avoids looking at their own part; the blamed partner becomes helpless, withdrawn and low in confidence.
- **Objectives:**
  - Keep discussions to present events (not old grievances) in all joint sessions for 6 weeks.
  - Use "I" statements rather than "you always…" in at least 3 logged conversations per week.
  - Each does 2 things per week that please the other.
  - The blaming partner writes a list of 5 positive changes they'd like (not faults) and acknowledges each change they notice.
  - Together write down the unspoken "rules" of the relationship whose breaking triggers blame, and renegotiate at least 2, by week 8.
  - List outside stresses (work, money, family) that feed blame, and problem-solve 1 of them.

**CP-INT Rigidity and intolerance**
- **Presentation:** each is convinced they're right; constant nit-picking; pushing to change the other; only anger and criticism are expressed.
- **Goals:** accept and tolerate differences; express softer feelings; meet some needs outside the relationship.
- **Objectives:**
  - Map the couple's polarising cycle together by week 4.
  - Each names 1 way they have been trying to force the other to change, by week 5.
  - Each identifies when their *reaction to* a problem has become a problem in itself, in at least 2 sessions.
  - Speak only for oneself (no mind-reading) — therapist notes at least 80% compliance in sessions by week 8.
  - Name the soft feeling under anger in 2 logged conflicts per week.
  - Complete 1 tolerance-building exercise (CPL-09) by week 10.
  - Each completes a My-Part Plan every fortnight.

**CP-DIS Disillusionment and drifting apart**
- **Presentation:** living separate lives; dwelling on past disappointments; imagining life outside the relationship.
- **Goals:** recommit; build on positives; renegotiate expectations; face challenges as a team.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each names 3 changes *they* will make, by week 3.
  - Each writes down what they expected from the relationship at the start (spoken and unspoken), then discuss which expectations still fit, by week 6.
  - Each writes about 1 hope that was fulfilled and 1 that wasn't, and shares them in session.
  - Together write 2 short accounts of their shared life: one about what has stayed steady, one about times they faced change together.
  - Each describes 1 way the other has tried to cope with life pressures, with empathy.
  - List which needs are met inside the relationship and which outside it.
  - Write a new relationship agreement (roles, expectations, how to handle problems) by week 12.

**CP-LOVE Loss of affection**
- **Presentation:** little warmth; no expressions of love; little kindness or touch; infrequent sex; avoiding each other.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each states a commitment to trying for an agreed period (e.g., 12 weeks), by week 2.
  - Complete at least 3 caring actions per week each, for 8 weeks.
  - Share 1 appreciation each per day.
  - Schedule 1 enjoyable shared activity per week.
  - Discuss realistic expectations about when feelings may return, in session by week 4.
  - Begin sensate focus (CPL-11) only when both agree and after at least 4 weeks of improved positives.
- **⚠:** no pressure for sex; affection usually returns after the relationship improves.

**CP-PERS Personality differences**
- **Presentation:** conflict over differences such as introversion vs extraversion, active vs home-loving, religious vs not.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each lists the 3 differences that cause most conflict, by week 3.
  - Each agrees to adjust 1 habit, by week 6.
  - Each names 1 way the difference benefits the family (e.g., shows children variety), by week 6.
  - Each compliments the partner on a differing quality in front of family or friends at least once a month.

**CP-REC Disagreements over leisure time**
- **Objectives:**
  - Each describes preferred leisure activities while the other reflects back, in 1 session.
  - Plan 1 shared activity per week, varying length, for 8 weeks.
  - Each joins the other's interest once a month as a gift.
  - Keep own hobby to an agreed number of hours per week.
  - Split holiday choices roughly equally over the year.
  - Plan an occasional day centred on one partner's preferences, alternating.

**CP-JEAL Jealousy**
- **Presentation:** fear of losing the partner to someone else; accusations; checking up; controlling behaviour; feeling humiliated; outbursts.
- **Goals:** no unfounded accusations, controlling or retaliatory behaviour; rebuild trust.
- **Objectives:**
  - Together distinguish, with examples, between understandable and unreasonable jealousy, by week 3.
  - The jealous partner writes a letter about their feelings and the couple discusses it using Clarity and Care skills.
  - The jealous partner practises a 10-minute worry-postponement or thought-defusion exercise when jealous thoughts intrude, logging use daily for 6 weeks.
  - Draw a timeline of when jealousy began and what has fuelled it, by week 5.
  - The jealous partner makes only specific, reasonable requests (e.g., "Please tell me if you'll be late"), and the couple agrees rules for contact with others, by week 8.
  - Reduce checking behaviour (phone, social media) to zero by week 8.
- **⚠:**
  - Screen for violence and coercive control — monitoring and restriction are red flags (CPL-20).
  - If jealousy seems delusional (morbid jealousy), refer for psychiatric assessment and risk assessment.
  - If jealousy is intense, begin with individual sessions.

### Betrayal and sexuality

**CP-INF After an affair (integrative approach; Baucom, Gordon & Snyder) — Evidence B**
- **Definition:** sexual *or* emotional intimacy with someone else, kept secret, that breaks the couple's spoken or unspoken agreement.
- **Stage 0 — setting up:**
  - Agree the type of therapy (CPL-03) and the no-secrets policy.
  - Safety agreements; screen for suicide and risk of harm.
  - **Agree to hold off on detailed discussion of the affair at home** in the early weeks.
  - Teach time-out (CPL-07).
- **Stage 1 — dealing with the impact:**
  - Individual sessions to explore guilt, anger, shame and mixed feelings.
  - Each writes a letter expressing their feelings, revises it to bring out softer emotions, and reads it in a joint session.
  - Coach the partner who had the affair: the hurt partner is usually most able to engage when the other shows (a) **understanding of what their behaviour meant and did** and (b) **genuine remorse**.
  - Normalise trauma-like reactions (intrusive thoughts, checking, low mood); teach ways to manage intrusive thoughts.
  - My-Part Plans for handling emotional triggers.
- **Stage 2 — making sense of it:**
  - Both list factors that contributed — in the relationship, in each person, and outside. Understanding is not excusing; responsibility for the affair remains with the partner who had it.
  - Review how the relationship was working before the affair.
  - The therapist writes a **shared formulation** of how the affair came about.
  - Daily Positives Log; desired changes; list of issues to work on later.
- **Stage 3 — moving forward:**
  - **Forgiveness as a process** (not excusing or forgetting).
  - An explicit decision about staying together, with pros and cons.
  - Rebuild trust area by area; agree boundaries with others.
  - Optional **ritual** marking a new chapter.
  - Or a respectful separation.
- **Example objectives:**
  - Complete the individual feelings letter by week 4 and read it jointly by week 6.
  - The partner who had the affair describes in writing what the affair meant and its effects, by week 8.
  - Complete the shared formulation by week 12.
  - Reach an explicit decision about the relationship by week 20 (or agree a new review date).
- **⚠:** if the affair is ongoing, apply the no-secrets policy; recommend STI testing; watch for violence after disclosure.

**CP-NET Pornography and online sexual activity**
- **Presentation:** secret use of pornography; the other partner feels betrayed, anxious, jealous or angry, sleeps badly, checks devices, considers leaving.
- **Goals:** agree what, if any, use of sexually explicit material is acceptable to both; agree boundaries; apology; understanding of the other's distress; rebuild trust or separate amicably.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each describes their moral and religious position on sexually explicit material in session, by week 3.
  - The therapist summarises current research evidence neutrally in 1 session.
  - The partner who used acknowledges the other's hurt and anxiety in their own words, by week 4.
  - Agree a device-transparency arrangement acceptable to both, by week 5.
  - Agree a written plan about future use by week 8.
  - Ensure children cannot access explicit material (parental controls) by week 2.
  - The partner who used identifies needs the use may have been meeting, in at least 2 sessions.
- **⚠:** screen for compulsive sexual behaviour disorder (ICD-11 6C72); illegal content requires legal action; online affairs → CP-INF.

**CP-SEXD Sexual difficulties**
- **Presentation:** low desire, aversion, arousal difficulties (erection, lubrication), difficulty reaching orgasm, pain with sex or vaginismus, often with relationship distress, criticism and low self-esteem.
- **Goals:** more desire and enjoyment; comfortable arousal; orgasm; no pain; better sexual communication; higher self-esteem; accept normal variation and changes over life.
- **Objectives:**
  - Medical assessment for physical causes and medication effects within 4 weeks.
  - Each shares family and religious messages about sex in session by week 4.
  - Complete sensate focus step 1 at least twice a week for 3 weeks before progressing.
  - For vaginismus, complete graded self-controlled dilator practice (after gynaecological assessment) 4 times a week, progressing one size every 1–2 weeks as comfortable.
  - Read agreed educational material and discuss it together by week 6.
- **Methods:** CPL-11 in full; explore trauma (pause triggering activities), body image, attraction to others, and same-sex feelings openly and without judgement.
- **Codes:** ICD-11 sexual dysfunctions HA00–HA03 (sexual pain HA20) · ICD-10-CM F52.x.

**CP-SXAB Sexual coercion within the relationship** → apply CPL-20/21, plus:
- Ground rules (no threats to leave or to have sex with others; no aggression).
- The person affected describes the coercion and its effects, in individual sessions.
- Psycho-education on power, control and consent.
- The partner who coerced explores contributing factors (e.g., own history of abuse, hostility, insecurity) — without this reducing responsibility.
- Trauma screening for both.
- Sensate focus only after safety is well established.
- Consider alternatives and a safe exit.

### Life stage and context

**CP-BLEND Blended / step-family**
- **Presentation:** disputes about discipline; favouritism; money connected to previous relationships; rivalry between step-siblings; worries about step-sibling or step-parent boundaries; access and contact logistics.
- **Objectives:**
  - Put access, childcare and transport arrangements in writing by week 4.
  - Draw up a budget covering costs arising from previous relationships by week 6.
  - The biological parent leads discipline; the step-parent focuses on building a relationship, with at least 1 one-to-one activity with each step-child per fortnight.
  - Discuss money and discipline only in private, never in front of the children, for 8 weeks.
  - Speak respectfully about ex-partners in front of the children at all times.
  - Plan 1 whole-family activity per month.
  - Ask the children about good times with their other parent at least once a month.
- **⚠:** any concern about sexual abuse of a child → Core 2 CHD-13 / POCSO.

**CP-PAR-C Disagreements about parenting young children** (informed by social-learning parenting programmes such as Triple P)
- **Common reasons for misbehaviour:**
  - To gain attention (happens mainly when someone is watching) → planned ignoring of minor misbehaviour
  - To escape or get something (avoid a task, win a treat) → calm, consistent follow-through
  - For its own sake (done out of sight because it's fun) → supervision and clear instructions
- **Objectives:**
  - Track 1–2 target behaviours with ABC records for 2 weeks.
  - Agree 5 house rules and consequences, written down, by week 4.
  - Each parent gives at least 5 specific praises per day to the child for 6 weeks.
  - Hold a 10-minute parenting check-in on at least 5 days a week (logged).
  - Use agreed time-out consistently for serious misbehaviour.
  - Each parent stops 1 behaviour they model that they don't want copied, by week 6.
  - Back each other's parenting decisions in front of the children; discuss disagreements privately.
- See Core 2 INT-20.

**CP-PAR-A Disagreements about parenting adolescents** (after Patterson & Forgatch; Robin & Foster)
- **Objectives:**
  - Identify parenting, family and school contributors in session by week 3.
  - Agree how the teenager's whereabouts will be monitored, by week 3.
  - Each parent notices and reinforces 1 positive behaviour per day.
  - Plan 1 enjoyable activity with the teenager per week.
  - Agree consequences for specific problem behaviours, in writing, by week 5.
  - Hold a weekly family problem-solving meeting for 8 weeks.
  - Use the Outcome-Check worksheet for at least 2 difficult parenting episodes.

**CP-TTP Becoming parents**
- **Presentation:** disagreements about the baby's sleep, feeding and crying; more arguments; negative thinking; feeling unsupported; overload; sex not yet resumed.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each completes a My-Part Plan every fortnight for 8 weeks.
  - Practise speaker–listener once a week and agree "support or solve?" before each talk.
  - Each thanks the other for a specific piece of baby care daily.
  - Schedule 30 minutes of couple time per week (with help if needed).
  - Problem-solve 1 baby-care issue per fortnight.
  - Talk about hopes for affection and sex in session by week 8, without pressure.
- **⚠:** screen both parents for **perinatal depression** (e.g., Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) and check infant safety (Core 1 ASM-06). Couple-based prevention programmes (e.g., Family Foundations; Feinberg) have supporting evidence.

**CP-DIV Separation and divorce**
- **Goals:** decide thoughtfully; **put the children's interests first**; manage anger; cope with losses; reach a fair agreement; keep conflict low; co-parent well.
- **Objectives:**
  - Both describe the history of the relationship, dissatisfaction and steps already taken, by week 3.
  - Complete individual violence screening by week 2.
  - Each writes pros and cons of staying and discusses cultural and religious views on divorce, by week 4.
  - Choose relationship, decision or separation therapy by week 5.
  - Each names 2 ways they helped and 2 ways they hurt the relationship, by week 8.
  - Plan and rehearse telling the children together; debrief afterwards.
  - Agree practical arrangements (who moves, when) in writing.
  - Prefer mediation over litigation where safe; attend at least 1 mediation session.
  - Complete a written **co-parenting plan** (residence, schedule, decision-making, communication rules) before separation.
  - Each rebuilds at least 2 social or spiritual supports.
- **India:** mutual-consent divorce (e.g., Hindu Marriage Act s. 13B; Special Marriage Act s. 28); family courts and court-linked mediation; custody decided on the child's welfare; stigma and family pressure. *Confirm the applicable personal law.*

**CP-FIN Conflict about money**
- **Objectives:**
  - Each describes their family's approach to money and any current parental influence, by week 3.
  - Each shares what they originally expected about income and spending.
  - Write a joint budget and long-term financial plan by week 8.
  - Agree whether and how each will earn, and how childcare is balanced with work, by week 8.
  - Write a debt-repayment plan if relevant.
  - If one partner controls all money, move to shared decisions on all spending above an agreed amount by week 10 — and look at control issues beyond money.
  - Screen for individual problems affecting money (addiction, gambling, mania, hoarding).
- **⚠:** **financial abuse** is a form of partner violence (CPL-20). India: dowry demands → legal referral.

**CP-JOB Work stress, job loss or reduced hours**
- **Objectives:**
  - Rule out a primary mental-health condition; refer for medication review if indicated.
  - Consider medical leave where appropriate.
  - Assess the fit between the person's skills and their job.
  - The partner offers 1 specific form of support agreed in session.
  - Identify 1 training or retraining step within 2 months.
  - Set boundaries on work time at home (e.g., no work messages after 9 p.m.) for 6 weeks.
  - Reduce complaining about work at home to an agreed 15-minute daily slot.

**CP-ROLE Work–home role strain**
- **Objectives:**
  - Each describes how work spills into home and home into work, by week 3.
  - Each writes their top 5 values and compares how time is actually spent against them (values clarification), by week 5.
  - Each identifies family, cultural and gender influences on role expectations.
  - Each describes the other's dilemma with empathy in session.
  - Problem-solve how time is divided; write a **role agreement** (who does what, and how recurring problems are handled) by week 8.
  - When time is short, protect basic needs (sleep, meals, some rest) first.
- **India note:** gender expectations around housework and in-laws; dual-career couples.

**CP-LIFE Major life changes** (relocation, job change, a child's chronic illness, disability)
- **Objectives:**
  - Each rates their readiness to adapt (stage of change) by week 2.
  - List positive and negative coping so far.
  - List the day-to-day changes required, and short- and long-term goals.
  - **Sort each change into "can change" or "must accept"** by week 4.
  - Brainstorm at least 2 solutions for each changeable issue and plan one.
  - Each describes the best realistic outcome in 6 months.
  - Schedule at least 1 enjoyable shared activity and 1 individual stress-relief activity per week.

**CP-MID Midlife transition**
- **Presentation:** low mood, irritability, tearfulness, poor sleep; torn between the partner and someone else, or over career or religious values; worries about ageing and attractiveness.
- **Objectives:**
  - Use the Outcome-Check worksheet for 2 recurring worries.
  - Each writes their current values and goals by week 4.
  - Each identifies 1 goal that is no longer realistic, grieves it in session, and replaces it with a new realistic goal and a My-Part Plan.
  - Name 3 advantages of this stage of life.
  - Take 1 step towards financial security by week 8.
  - Increase affectionate touch and family time by an agreed amount.
  - Take on 1 generative or legacy activity (mentoring, volunteering, teaching a skill) within 3 months.
  - Explore career feelings: either accept the current role or plan a structured exploration.

**CP-RET Retirement**
- **Presentation:** low mood or anxiety; loss of routine and status; conflict about household roles now both are home; communication difficulties; changes in sex.
- **Objectives:**
  - Review the relationship's history and strengths in session.
  - Practise communication skills weekly (CPL-06).
  - Each writes values and goals for this life stage.
  - Create 3 activity lists (alone, together, with others) and schedule from each weekly.
  - Each takes on 1 volunteer or community role within 3 months.
  - Discuss sexual expectations and any medical factors; begin sensate focus if wanted.

**CP-REL Religious or spiritual differences**
- **Presentation:** disputes about beliefs and practices; one partner pressuring the other; reduced closeness; conflict about children's religious upbringing and discipline.
- **Objectives:**
  - Each describes how their faith strengthens the relationship and where it creates conflict.
  - Each promises not to pressure the other about beliefs, from week 2.
  - Each explains what commitment, respect, closeness, forgiveness and sexuality mean in their tradition, in session.
  - Each explains why agreement matters to them and how much flexibility they have.
  - Revisit any agreements about the children's religious upbringing and reach one both can support, by week 10.
- **India:** inter-faith and inter-caste marriages (Special Marriage Act, 1954); pressure from extended family; festivals and rituals; children's names and religion. Take the family system into account.

## PART 27 — INDIA ADAPTATION (CPL-30)
- **Arranged and semi-arranged marriages:**
  - Commitment may come before romantic love — this is not a problem in itself.
  - Assess each partner's freedom and consent in the choice.
  - Screen for forced or child marriage (Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006).
- **Joint and extended families:**
  - Conflict with in-laws is a core issue. Use a **boundary and alliance map** (Core 2 INT-19, structural).
  - With consent, consider involving key elders in some sessions.
  - Respect family hierarchy while protecting the couple's own space.
- **Stigma and help-seeking:** couples often arrive via a doctor (with physical complaints), a religious leader or family elders. Make confidentiality from the wider family explicit.
- **Gender norms:** housework, women's employment, decision-making power, dowry. Challenge abuse without imposing one model of roles.
- **Sexual health:** limited sex education; performance anxiety and beliefs about semen loss ("dhat"); vaginismus among newly married couples; family pressure around fertility. Psycho-education, sensate focus and medical referral are especially valuable.
- **Divorce:** strong stigma; worries about custody and maintenance; family-court mediation. Separation therapy may take longer.
- **Language:** offer sessions in the couple's preferred language. Use trained interpreters, never family members.
