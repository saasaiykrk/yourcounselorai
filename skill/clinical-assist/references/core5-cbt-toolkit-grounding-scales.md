## PART 28 — CBT WORKSHEET LIBRARY (WS)
All worksheets are designed for the app: every field has a data type, and ratings are 0–100% unless stated otherwise.

### WS-01 Thought-record family — choose by client readiness
| ID | Version | Fields | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| WS-01a | **Starter record** (4 columns + 1) | What happened · What went through my mind · What I felt · What I did · Another way to see it | Beginners, adolescents, lower literacy, the first 1–2 weeks |
| WS-01b | **Standard automatic-thought record** (in the tradition of Beck's Daily Record of Dysfunctional Thoughts) | Date/time · Situation and body sensations · Automatic thoughts (belief %) · Emotions (intensity %) · Balanced response (thinking traps named; belief %) · Result (re-rate thoughts and emotions %) | Routine CBT for depression and anxiety |
| WS-01c | **Evidence record** (7 fields) | Trigger · Emotions and body sensations (%) · Thoughts or images · Evidence that supports the key thought · Evidence that doesn't fit it · Balanced view · Result (re-rate %; what I'll do next time) | Clients ready to weigh evidence and work on the most emotionally charged thought |
| WS-01d | **Action record** | Situation · Emotions (%) · Body sensations · Thoughts or images · Balanced or defused thought · What I did / the most helpful thing I could do · Re-rate % | Adds a behaviour step; useful for anger, social anxiety and depression (activation) |

**Prompt bank (original wording for app tooltips):**
- **Situation:** Describe it as a camera would — where were you, when, with whom, what happened? Leave out opinions for now.
- **Emotions:** Name each feeling and rate its strength 0–100. Was there more than one?
- **Body:** What did you notice physically, and where?
- **Thoughts:**
  - What words or pictures came into your mind?
  - What does this seem to say about you, other people, or what lies ahead?
  - What is the worst you feared might happen?
  - → Mark the **key thought** — the one tied to the strongest feeling.
- **Evidence:**
  - What facts back up the key thought?
  - What facts don't fit with it, or show it isn't the whole story?
  - Which parts are facts and which are interpretations?
  - What would someone who knows you well say?
- **Finding a balanced response — four questions:**
  1. What does the evidence on both sides show?
  2. What other explanations are possible?
  3. What is the worst, the best and the most likely outcome — and could I cope with the worst?
  4. What would I say to a friend who had this thought?
- **PAUSE — a quick in-the-moment version (YourCounselor AI):**
  - **P**ause — stop before reacting
  - **A**nchor — one slow breath, feet on the floor
  - **U**nderstand — notice the thought, feeling and body sensation
  - **S**tep back — look at the wider picture ("How much will this matter next month? How would someone else see it?")
  - **E**ngage wisely — choose the response that fits your values and goals
- **Defusion phrases** (ACT; Core 3 PDX-05): "My mind is telling me a worry story again." · "I'm noticing the thought that…" · "This feeling makes sense given what I've been through, and it will pass."
- **Best response:** What could I do differently? Which option would work best? What is likely to follow from it?

**Worked examples (fictional, for training content):**

| Situation | Emotion before | Key thought | Balanced thought | Action | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colleague doesn't reply to a friendly message all day | Hurt 70%, anxious 60% | "She's annoyed with me; I must have said something wrong" | "She had back-to-back meetings today. If something were wrong, she'd probably tell me" | Gets on with work; says hello the next morning as usual | ≈20% |
| Wakes at noon on a day off with a list of chores undone | Low 90% | "I'm hopeless — I can't even manage a Sunday" | "Low energy is part of depression, not a character flaw. One small task still counts" | Showers, does one chore, messages a sibling | 55% |
| Shopkeeper serves the person behind them first | Angry 80% | "He's disrespecting me deliberately" | "The shop is busy and he may not have seen who came first. It's a two-minute delay" | Says calmly, "I think I was next," then lets it go | 15% |

**Scoring and app logic:**
- Emotion change = before − after.
- Change in belief in the key thought.
- Count thinking traps tagged (Core 1 INT-04 list).
- Show weekly trends on the dashboard.

### WS-02 Working with core beliefs (usually after 3–6 weeks of thought records)
**WS-02a Quick core-belief finder:**
1. Finish these sentences quickly, without editing: "I am…", "Other people are…", "The world is…".
2. Reflect: What feelings come with these? When do you first remember believing them? What experiences shaped them? Does anyone close to you see things the same way?
3. **Are these beliefs still useful to you?** Write one more balanced belief for each (self, others, world).
4. When the old belief colours an event, remind yourself where it came from and **look at the event again through the balanced belief**.

**WS-02b Core-belief formulation (connecting the surface to the core):**

| Field | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|
| Situation | Prepared a presentation for two evenings, then decided it was "rubbish" and asked a colleague to present instead |
| Automatic thought | "I'll make a fool of myself in front of everyone" |
| Emotion | Dread, shame |
| **Underlying theme** (downward arrow) | "People will see I'm not as capable as they think" |
| **Possible core belief** | "I'm incompetent" |
| **Formative experience** | Being publicly criticised by a teacher at school |
| **Rule for living that followed** | "If I avoid being seen, I can't be exposed" |
| **Unhelpful coping** | Handing visible tasks to others; declining promotions |
| **Alternative coping** | Presenting to a small, friendly group first; asking for specific feedback; noting what went well |

**Downward-arrow technique:** after the key thought, ask "If that were true, what would it mean about me?" and repeat until a broad statement about the self appears — commonly in themes of helplessness, unlovability or worthlessness (Beck; J. S. Beck). *(Equivalent to REBT inference chaining, Core 2 INT-15.)*

**WS-02c Positive evidence log** (method described by Padesky; see also Greenberger & Padesky's *Mind Over Mood*):
- Write the **new, balanced core belief**.
- Over the following weeks and months, record small everyday experiences that fit the new belief — aim for at least one entry a day.
- Rate how strongly you believe the new belief (0–100%) once a week.
- **Why this matters:** long-held core beliefs filter experience — we notice what fits them and overlook what doesn't. A deliberate log helps correct that bias.

**Complementary techniques:**
- **Continuum:** rate yourself 0–100 on the quality in question, using real people as reference points; this loosens all-or-nothing beliefs.
- **Historical review:** gather evidence for and against the belief across different periods of life.
- **Responsibility pie chart:** for guilt — share responsibility across all the contributing factors.
- **Behavioural experiments:** prediction → test → result → what I learned.

## PART 29 — GROUNDING TECHNIQUES (GRD)
- **When to use:** flashbacks, dissociation, panic, overwhelming emotion, urges to self-harm. Teach grounding before any trauma work; it also serves as a distress-tolerance skill (Core 3 PDX-01).
- **Evidence:** B–C (a standard part of trauma-stabilisation approaches).
- **Level:** L1 may teach these.

| ID | Type | Procedure (YourCounselor AI wording) |
|---|---|---|
| GRD-01 | **5-4-3-2-1 senses** | Look around and name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can physically feel (e.g., your back against the chair, your feet on the floor), 3 sounds you can hear, 2 things you can smell (or 2 smells you like), and 1 thing you can taste — or 1 kind thing about yourself |
| GRD-02 | **Personal anchor card** | When calm, create a small card (paper or in-app) with one comforting anchor for each sense — e.g., a photo you love, the feel of a smooth stone, a favourite song, the smell of cardamom tea, the taste of a mint. Keep it with you. When distressed, breathe slowly and go through each anchor in turn |
| GRD-03 | **Physical / sensory** | Keep eyes open and describe the room in detail · hold something with texture (a cushion, a stress ball) · a cool damp cloth on the face or a cold drink in the hands · press feet firmly into the floor · play calming music · focus on a trusted person's voice |
| GRD-04 | **Orientation** | Answer out loud: Where am I right now? What day and date is it? What month and year? How old am I? What season is it? Finish with: "I am here, now, and safe. That was then." |
| GRD-05 | **Other standard options** | Slow breathing (in for 4, out for 6–8) · naming 10 items in a category (fruits, cities, cricketers) · describing an object in detail · counting backwards in 7s from 100 · repeating a personal grounding phrase or picturing a safe place |

**Rules:**
- Keep your eyes open.
- Practise when calm first, so the skill is ready when needed.
- Rate distress 0–10 before and after.
- Check with a doctor before using ice or cold-water methods if there is a heart condition or an eating disorder.

**App:** a one-tap "Ground me" button that runs a guided 5-4-3-2-1 or orientation routine, records distress before and after, and shows crisis numbers if distress remains at 8 or above (Core 1 ASM-06).

## PART 30 — CBT SESSION QUALITY: YOURCOUNSELOR CHECKLIST (FID)
**Purpose:** structured feedback on how well a CBT session was delivered — for supervision, training and internal quality assurance.

**Important licensing note:** the established competence scales — the Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale (CTRS; Young & Beck, 1980), its revised rubric from the Beck Institute, and the Revised Cognitive Therapy Scale (CTS-R; Blackburn et al., 2001) — are copyrighted instruments. v1 of this Core paraphrased a proprietary rubric item by item; that material has been removed. What follows is an **original in-house checklist (FID-YC)** built from widely recognised components of a CBT session (see also the UCL CBT competence framework; Roth & Pilling, 2007). It is suitable for internal development feedback. **For formal certification or research, license and use the CTRS or CTS-R directly.**

- **Items:** 12, grouped into three areas.
- **Rating for each item:**
  - 0 = missing
  - 1 = attempted but with significant problems
  - 2 = done competently
  - 3 = done skilfully and flexibly
- **Total:** 0–36. **Provisional "competent" threshold: 24 or above, with no item scored 0** (IH — calibrate locally against supervisor judgement before relying on it).

| # | Area | Item | What "2 — competent" looks like | What moves it to "3 — skilful" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structure | **Check-in and link** | Brief mood check (ideally with a rating or measure) and a link back to the last session | Mood data are used to shape the session's priorities |
| 2 | Structure | **Shared agenda** | 1–3 concrete items agreed together near the start | Items ranked by importance; any change of plan is openly agreed |
| 3 | Structure | **Home-practice review** | Last session's practice is reviewed, including what got in the way | Learning from the practice is drawn out and connected to today's work |
| 4 | Structure | **Time management** | Session runs about 45–55 minutes with time for each agenda item and for closing | Moves smoothly between parts; redirects gently from unproductive detours |
| 5 | Relationship | **Understanding** | Regular accurate reflections and summaries of what the client says | Also names the client's feelings accurately and links them to the formulation |
| 6 | Relationship | **Warmth and respect** | Shows genuine care, reinforces the client's efforts, stays professional | Consistently warm and non-judgemental; recognises the client's strengths and values |
| 7 | Relationship | **Collaboration** | Client has real input into the agenda, the choice of methods and the practice task | Joint decision-making feels natural throughout the session |
| 8 | Change process | **Focus on what matters** | Draws out specific thoughts, images, feelings and behaviours linked to the agenda item | Targets the thought or behaviour with the greatest potential impact |
| 9 | Change process | **Guided discovery** | Uses questions to help the client examine their thinking, without lecturing or pressuring | The client reaches new conclusions themselves, and the effect on feelings and behaviour is checked |
| 10 | Change process | **Method choice** | Selects at least one evidence-based CBT method that fits the problem and explains why | Offers options and gains the client's agreement to the method |
| 11 | Change process | **Method delivery** | The method is carried out recognisably and could plausibly produce change | Delivered accurately and confidently, adapted to this client |
| 12 | Structure | **New home practice, summary and feedback** | A practice task is agreed that follows from the session; the session is summarised; the client's feedback is invited | The task specifies what, when, where and how often; likely obstacles are problem-solved; feedback is invited at several points, not only at the end |

**Pre-session reminder card (therapist):**
1. Check-in, mood rating, link to last session
2. Agree the agenda
3. Review home practice
4. For each agenda item: find the key thought or behaviour → guided discovery → choose and apply a method → check what shifted
5. Summarise along the way
6. Agree new home practice (specific; obstacles planned for)
7. Final summary and client feedback

Fits Core 2 CSK-31 and Core 2 INT-14. Target length 45–55 minutes.

**App features:**
- Supervisors rate recorded sessions (with the client's written consent; Core 1 ETH).
- Radar chart across the 12 items.
- Threshold indicator (provisional ≥ 24, no zero items).
- Links from low items to training content (e.g., low item 9 → Socratic questioning, Core 1 INT-04).
- Comparison of self-rating with supervisor rating.
- **Suggested local rule:** progression from L1 to L2 supervised practice after 3 consecutive recordings rated at or above the threshold by a qualified supervisor.

```
SessionQualityRating {rating_id, clinician_id, session_ref, rater_id, rater_role: self|supervisor|peer,
  instrument: FID-YC|CTRS|CTS-R (licensed), items[]{score_0_3, comment}, total,
  meets_threshold: bool, date}
```
*(v1 → v2: FidelityRating renamed SessionQualityRating; items[11] → items[12]; `instrument` field added so a licensed CTRS/CTS-R can be recorded when available.)*

## PART 31 — THEORY COMPARISON MATRIX (THY)
Quick reference for case formulation and for the app's "approach selector". Each row is our own summary; deeper cards are cross-referenced.

| Approach | Core view of people | Where problems come from | Aim of change | Typical methods | See |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Behavioural | Behaviour is learned and can be relearned | Learning history; reinforcement; skills missing or overused | Change behaviour and what maintains it | ABC analysis, reinforcement, shaping, exposure, token systems | Core 1 INT-03 |
| Bibliotherapy | People can gain insight and skills through guided reading | Gaps in knowledge or perspective | Understanding, normalising, new options | Selected reading **followed by discussion** | Core 2 INT-16; Core 3 PDX-14 |
| Cognitive-behavioural | People can learn to notice and reshape their thinking | Biased or unhelpful ways of processing events | More balanced thinking and helpful behaviour | Identifying thoughts, reframing, experiments, home practice, journalling, role-play | Core 1 INT-04; WS above |
| **Conjoint family (Satir)** | Everyone has the capacity to learn and grow | Low self-worth leading to unclear, defensive communication; rigid family rules | Higher self-worth; honest, congruent communication; flexible rules | Clarify vague words ("respect", "support"); make expectations explicit; build every member's self-worth; teach congruent communication; members own their actions | THY-01 |
| **Existential** | People seek meaning; they are free and responsible, and both alone and connected | Avoiding freedom and responsibility; loss of meaning; anxiety about death | Awareness, authentic choice, meaning | Reflection, therapist openness, confronting avoidance, exploring values; Frankl's meaning-centred work | THY-02 |
| **Gestalt** | People function as wholes and seek completion | Unfinished business; lack of present awareness; absorbed "shoulds" | Awareness, contact and integration | Empty chair (Core 1 INT-05), "What are you aware of right now?", owning language ("I" rather than "it") | Core 1 INT-05 |
| Narrative | Meaning is created through the stories people tell | Stories dominated by the problem | Re-author a preferred story | **Externalising**, finding unique outcomes, therapeutic letters | Core 1 INT-06; Core 2 INT-19 |
| **Paradoxical / brief strategic** | People often resist direct pressure to change | Attempts to fix the problem keep it going | Interrupt the pattern | Positioning, prescribing the symptom, reframing — ⚠ L3 only | Core 2 INT-19 |
| Person-centred (Rogers) | People are trustworthy and move towards growth | Gap between self-concept and actual experience | Congruence and self-direction | Therapist genuineness, unconditional positive regard, accurate empathy | Core 2 INT-13; CSK-11 |
| Play / art | Children and less verbal clients express themselves through play and creative media | Experiences not yet expressed | Expression and mastery | Play and art materials | Core 2 INT-13 |
| Psychodynamic | Much behaviour is shaped by processes outside awareness | Unconscious conflict and defences | Insight; bringing hidden patterns into awareness | Free association, work with transference and resistance, interpretation | Core 1 INT-02; Core 3 PDX-04 |
| REBT (Ellis) | People think both rationally and irrationally and can choose | Rigid demands, catastrophising, condemning self or others | A flexible, accepting philosophy | Active disputing; rational alternatives | Core 1 INT-04; Core 2 INT-15 |
| Reality / choice (Glasser) | People choose behaviour to meet basic needs | Ineffective choices for meeting needs | Take charge of present behaviour | WDEP: wants → current doing → evaluation → plan and commitment | Core 2 INT-16 |
| Solution-focused | Clients have resources; focus on the present and future | Repeating what doesn't work | Small changes built on exceptions | Miracle question, scaling, exceptions, "do something different" | Core 2 INT-17 |
| Strategic | Families form patterns that keep problems going | Unhelpful sequences and hierarchy | Change the pattern; strengthen parental leadership | Directives, new behaviours, focus on the presenting problem | Core 2 INT-19 |
| **Planning-focused (students / careers)** | People may lack long-term planning skills | Disconnected resources; no plan | Connect to resources; build life and coping skills | Try several strategies; **set time frames**; break big goals down; **plan B** | THY-03 |
| Structural (Minuchin) | Families are systems with rules and subsystems | Boundary and hierarchy problems | Reorganise the family structure; shift focus off the "identified patient" | Joining, enactment, boundary-making | Core 2 INT-19 |

### THY-01 Satir's communication stances
Satir described how, under stress, people often protect their sense of worth by communicating in ways that leave something out:

| Stance | What gets left out | How it looks | Therapeutic aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Placating** | Own needs and feelings | Agreeing, apologising, trying to please | Voice own needs |
| **Blaming** | The other person | Fault-finding, controlling | Recognise the other's feelings |
| **Super-reasonable** | Feelings — own and others' | Detached, intellectual, rule-bound | Notice and express feelings |
| **Irrelevant** | Self, other and the situation | Changing the subject, joking, distracting | Stay present and on topic |
| **Congruent** | Nothing — self, other and context all respected | Words, tone and body match | **The goal stance** |

**Other Satir methods:**
- Family sculpting (placing members physically to show relationships)
- Family reconstruction
- Clarifying vague words ("What would I see if you felt respected?")
- Building self-worth in every family member

**Use:** couple and family work (Core 4 CPL-06) and assertiveness training.

### THY-02 Existential therapy essentials
- **Four "givens" of existence (Yalom):** death, freedom and responsibility, isolation, meaninglessness.
- **Existential anxiety** is a normal response to these givens; problems arise when people organise their lives around avoiding them.
- **Methods:**
  - Explore the client's choices and responsibility ("Whose choice is this?").
  - Gently confront avoidance.
  - Use awareness of mortality to clarify priorities.
  - Meaning-making, drawing on Frankl's three routes to meaning — what we create or give, what we experience or love, and the stance we take towards suffering we cannot avoid.
  - Authentic, here-and-now relationship.
- **Evidence:** C overall (meaning-centred psychotherapy has **B**-level evidence in advanced cancer care; Breitbart and colleagues).
- **Links:** midlife and retirement (Core 4 CP-MID / CP-RET); SR-22 and SR-24 (registry).

### THY-03 Planning-focused counselling (students and careers)
1. Try out several strategies and keep what works.
2. Set **specific time frames**.
3. Break large goals into smaller steps (Core 2 CSK-32, SMART).
4. Prepare a **plan B** for each key goal.
5. Connect the person with resources (mentors, services, financial aid).
6. Teach life and coping skills.
7. Review progress.

Fits Core 1 CNS-02.

## PART 32 — SELF-REPORT SCALE REGISTRY (SR)

### SR-00 How this registry works
- **What changed from v1:** v1 contained scoring keys, reverse-scored item lists, subscale item assignments and percentile norms extracted from a published compilation of personality tests. That compilation reprinted each scale with its author's permission, and its norm tables are part of the published work. **All keys and norms have been removed.** The registry now records only what you need to find, evaluate and license each scale.
- **Licence code: PERM** — items, keys and norms may be used only with written permission from the author or copyright holder (or under a published free-use statement). Once permission is obtained, load the definition from the **primary source** into `ScaleDefinition` (schema below).
- **Norms:** most of these scales were normed on US or Western student and community samples decades ago. Even when licensed, treat norms as rough guides, flag this in reports, and build **Indian local norms** (Core 1 PSY-10).
- **Purpose:** self-understanding and screening only (evidence C–B as used here), **not diagnosis**. Pair any concern with an interview and a validated clinical measure (Core 1 INS).
- **Free alternatives column:** where a validated alternative appears to be freely available, it is listed. ⚠ **Verify each licence before use** — "free for research" often excludes commercial apps. The **International Personality Item Pool (IPIP)** is in the public domain and is a safe first choice for personality traits.
- **Reporting rule (once licensed):** report results as broad bands rather than exact percentiles, and present them as a starting point for reflection, not a verdict. Pick one target, work on it, and re-test in 8–12 weeks.

```
  response_format, licence: PERM|FREE_RESEARCH|PD, licence_status: not_requested|requested|granted|denied,
  licence_doc_ref, free_alternative, clinical_use_notes}
ScaleDefinition {id, version, source: licensed_primary, reverse_items[], subscales{}, weights{}, key{},
  norms{…}, norm_sample_note, loaded_by, loaded_at}   // created only when licence_status = granted
ScaleResult {client_id, scale_id, date, raw_by_subscale{}, band{}, entered_by: client|clinician}
```
**Product rule:** the app refuses to administer or score an SR scale unless `licence_status = granted` and a `ScaleDefinition` sourced from the licensed primary publication exists.
