# Referral criteria (used by v2.1 Section 16)
Each referral line states **who**, **why** and **urgency**: **Emergency** (now — call 112 / go to emergency) · **Urgent** (within 24–72 hours) · **Soon** (within 1–2 weeks) · **Routine**.
*India crisis numbers are maintained in `crisis-resources.md` (owner: Clinical Safety Officer; annual review). Confirm local pathways before release.*

## 1. Emergency (same day)
- Suicide attempt, or ideation with plan, intent and access to means (Core 1 ASM-06)
- Imminent risk of harm to others; credible threat to an identifiable person (duty to protect, Core 1 ETH-01)
- Acute psychosis or mania with danger, severe agitation or inability to care for self
- Delirium or acute confusion; suspected overdose or poisoning; severe alcohol withdrawal (tremor, confusion, seizures)
- Medically unstable eating disorder (fainting, chest pain, very low weight, refusal of all fluids)
- Child in immediate danger, or disclosure of ongoing sexual abuse → child protection / police / 1098 (POCSO; Core 2 CHD-13)
- Partner violence with fear, strangulation, weapons or threats to kill → safety plan + police / 181 (Core 4 CPL-21)

## 2. Psychiatry
- **Urgent:** new psychotic symptoms; possible bipolar episode; severe depression with marked physical symptoms or not eating/drinking; moderate risk not reducing with outpatient safety planning
- **Soon:** moderate–severe depression, anxiety or OCD where medication may help, or combined treatment is indicated; comorbid substance dependence
- **Routine:** limited response to an adequate psychological trial (e.g., no reliable change by the planned review); medication review or side-effects; diagnostic clarification of complex presentations

## 3. Medical (GP / physician / paediatrician / neurologist)
- **Soon:** sudden onset of OCD or tics in a child after infection (consider PANS/PANDAS); new cognitive decline, especially after 50 (Core 6 NPS-05); head injury with ongoing symptoms; seizures; unexplained weight change; physical symptoms not yet medically assessed; suspected thyroid, B12 or other medical contributors; sexual dysfunction (medical causes first, Core 4 CP-SEXD); perinatal mood problems (with obstetric team)
- **Routine:** regular physical health checks; medication interactions (including traditional remedies)

## 4. Neuropsychology / developmental
- **Routine:** suspected autism, ADHD, intellectual disability or specific learning disorder (Core 1 INS-05; Core 2 CHD-30s); cognitive complaints needing full testing; fitness-to-work or capacity questions (L3)

## 5. Specialist psychological services
- Complex trauma, dissociation, eating disorders, personality difficulties with high risk (DBT programme), perpetrator programmes, sex therapy, specialist addiction services — when the case exceeds the clinician's competence (Core 2 CSK-14)

## 6. School, social and legal
- **School liaison (with consent):** accommodations, bullying, school refusal, attendance plans (RPwD Act accommodations)
- **Social services / NGOs:** housing, finances, caregiver support, peer-support groups
- **Legal:** Protection Officer (PWDVA 2005), legal aid, family court counselling or mediation

## 7. Level rule
- **L1 users:** any referral beyond routine school or social liaison is discussed with a supervisor first; in emergencies act first, then inform the supervisor.
